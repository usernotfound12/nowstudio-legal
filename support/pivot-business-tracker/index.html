What the app does

Pivot is a founder's showcase and a peer feed in one. You build a pitch deck as a swipeable carousel, discover other founders in the room over a direct phone-to-phone connection and — once you both agree to connect — see each other's decks. The Problems tab is where you post problems, file feedback on other startups, answer other people's problems and earn points.

Is there an account to create?

No. Pivot has no sign-up, no login and no server. Everything you enter is saved on the device you entered it on. That also means nothing syncs between your devices and there is nothing to restore if you move to a new phone — a new install starts empty.

Does Pivot remember what I type?

Yes. Your profile and pitch deck, your posts, likes, comments, your problems, feedback, responses, points and your access state are saved to files on your device and are still there next launch. An older version of this page said the app forgot everything when it closed; that is no longer how it behaves.

What does reset each launch is the sample material — the example founders, example feed posts and example nearby entries are re-seeded from inside the app and stay labelled as samples. Your own content sits alongside them and is kept.

My profile is empty when I open it

That is the intended starting state, not a loading failure. A new install ships with no name, no company, no headline numbers and no slides, so nothing invented ever ends up presented as yours. Fill in the profile and add slides and they become your deck. The polished example deck you can browse belongs to an invented founder, is labelled as an example, and is never copied into your profile.

How do I get into the Problems tab?

Tap Explore with Sample Access on the owner verification screen. That is the route that works.

The other route, applying to be a verified business owner, needs two approvals from other verified owners signed in on the same device. Pivot has no server, so applications never travel between phones, and one person per install means there is nobody who could vote. Rather than take an application and park it forever at nought of two, the app does not offer the form at all: it tells you verification is not running and why. Nothing approves itself in the background.

Sample Access opens the tab immediately with example content to read. Anything you post, answer or earn from there is genuinely yours and is saved on your device. You are badged “Sample Access” rather than verified wherever the badge appears, and you can turn it off again on the same screen.

Nearby discovery is not finding anyone

Nearby needs the local network permission, which iOS asks for the first time you switch discovery on, and it needs the other person to have Pivot open with discovery on as well. Both devices should have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth enabled, since Apple's peer-to-peer networking uses them to find each other even without a shared network. If the permission was declined, re-enable it in Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.

If you want to see what the screen looks like with people on it, tap Preview with sample founders. Those entries carry a sample badge and a standing banner; nobody is on the other end of them, so they cannot be connected to or beamed to.

Connecting to someone nearby

Both of you have to agree, and neither side happens automatically. Tap Connect on one person and they get a request showing the name and company you are advertising; they accept or decline it themselves. Only after that does anything cross — and at that point your full profile, including your deck and your headline numbers, is sent to that one device. Before anyone consents, all that is broadcast is your name, your company, your avatar colour and a random per-install identifier. An unanswered request declines itself after about twenty-five seconds.

Blocking and reporting someone

Both are on the person's card in Nearby, and both are collected in Nearby › Safety. Blocking is the control that actually stops someone reaching you: they are filtered off your radar and their requests are refused before you ever see them, and the block is kept between launches.

A report is a record on your phone, not a submission. Pivot has no server, so filing one does not send anything to us or to anyone else. It saves a dated entry — the reason, your note, the name and company they were claiming, and an excerpt of what they sent — which you can read, share yourself or delete from Nearby › Safety. If you also want them gone, tick the block option or block them separately.

What the $0.99 unlock buys

Pivot Pro adds the outbound half of Nearby: beaming your deck to someone you have connected with, and auto-beam on connect. Discovery, being discoverable, seeing who is around and reading their deck are all free, as is everything in the other tabs.

Restoring a purchase

Purchases are tied to your Apple Account. Reinstall, open the app and use Restore Purchases in Settings. If nothing appears, check you are signed in with the same Apple Account you bought it on.

A permission prompt was declined

Local Network can be re-enabled at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security. Until then Nearby discovery and beaming stay unavailable; the rest of the app works without it. It is the only permission Pivot asks for.

Starting over, and deleting your data

Settings › Reset my data erases your profile, deck, posts, comments and Problems activity and returns the app to first launch. Your purchase is not affected. It deliberately leaves your block list and your report log alone, because silently unblocking everyone would not be a reset — manage those in Nearby › Safety, which has Unblock on every entry and a clear-all for reports.

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your device. We hold nothing on our side, so there is nothing for us to delete.
