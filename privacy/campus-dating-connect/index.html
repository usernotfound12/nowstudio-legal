Campus Dating Connect Privacy Policy

Last updated 29 August 2026 · Now Studio LLC

The short version. This is a dating app, so it handles personal profile data. Two things are worth knowing before you sign up. First, your conversations and shared photos live in your own iCloud account and Now Studio cannot read them. Second, to join you must apply to the community, and your application is deliberately visible to other verified members so they can review it — that part is not private, and the rest of this page explains exactly what it contains.

Game Center sign-in is required

You cannot reach discovery or matching until you are signed in to Game Center. That is the app's anti-bot measure: a Game Center identity is tied to a real, unique Apple Account, so it is far harder to mass-produce than an email address.

  • What the app reads from Game Center — your Game Center display name and your team player ID (an identifier Apple scopes to this developer). In a live matchmaking session it also sees the other player's Game Center alias, and they see yours.
  • What it does not read — your Apple Account email address, your real name, your password, your friends list, your achievements, your scores or your game history. The app requests none of those and Apple does not hand them over.

The profile you create

Signup asks for a display name, a date of birth, a short bio and a set of interests. The date of birth is used to calculate your age and is not itself saved — only the resulting age number is. Your name, age, bio and interests are stored on your device and are what other people see.

Community vetting uses a public CloudKit database — please read this part

Joining is not automatic. When you finish signup, the app writes a PendingApplicant record into the public database of the app's CloudKit container. A CloudKit public database is shared by everyone using the app. That record contains:

  • your display name, your age, your bio and your profile image name;
  • your CloudKit user record identifier, an opaque id Apple issues for this app;
  • your application status, its approval and flag counts, and the date you applied;
  • if you joined with an invite code, the identifier of the member who issued it.

These fields are stored unencrypted on purpose. The whole point of the feature is that other verified members open a review queue, read your name, age and bio, and then approve or flag you. Three approvals from distinct members get you in; two flags reject you. So: anything you type into your name or bio at signup should be treated as visible to other members of the app, not as private. Approval and flag records are also written to the same public database and record which member approved or flagged which applicant, by opaque CloudKit id. Invite codes are stored there too, along with who issued each one and who redeemed it.

Because this is a public CloudKit database rather than the private per-user storage the rest of the app uses, Now Studio, as the owner of the CloudKit container, can see and administer these records through Apple's CloudKit Dashboard. We are stating that plainly rather than implying otherwise. The app has no in-app button that deletes your applicant record, and the record remains after a decision is made — if you want yours removed, email us and we will delete it.

Chats and shared photos stay in your own iCloud

Every match gets its own private CloudKit record zone, shared between exactly the two of you using Apple's CKShare. The person who created the match holds the zone in their private CloudKit database; the other person sees the same zone in their shared database. Both sit inside the two participants' own iCloud accounts under their own Apple Accounts. The share is invite-only — it has no public permission, so nobody can reach the conversation by finding the link.

  • Message text is written into CloudKit's encrypted fields, which Apple encrypts with keys held by the share's participants.
  • Sent alongside each message, and not encrypted, are an opaque sender identifier and a timestamp, which CloudKit needs to route and order the conversation.
  • Photos you send in a chat are uploaded to that same shared zone as CloudKit assets, and a copy is also written into the app's own folder on each participant's device. Photo attachments are stored as assets rather than in the encrypted-field set.
  • When a match is first created, a small introduction record carrying your display name, age, bio and profile image name is placed in the zone so the other person can see who they matched with. Only that one match participant can read it.

Now Studio is not a participant in any of these shares and has no access to them. We cannot read your messages or your shared photos.

Your voice intro: the recording is deleted, only the text is kept

Signup offers a short spoken intro of roughly fifteen to thirty seconds. Here is exactly what the app does with it. The audio is written to a temporary file, handed to Apple's Speech framework for transcription, and the file is then deleted the moment transcription finishes — including when transcription fails, and immediately if you cancel. Only the resulting text is kept, and it becomes your bio, which you can edit. The audio recording is never uploaded to Now Studio and is never attached to any CloudKit record.

One honest caveat about the transcription itself: the app asks for on-device recognition wherever the device and language support it, so in that case your voice never leaves your phone. Some devices and languages cannot do on-device recognition at all, and there the transcription is performed by Apple's speech recognition service under Apple's own privacy policy. Either way the recording is deleted afterwards and never reaches us.

Two permissions cover this, and these are the exact sentences you will see on the prompts:

  • Microphone — “Your voice intro is transcribed on this device during signup. The recording itself is deleted immediately afterward and is never uploaded.”
  • Speech recognition — “Speech recognition turns your recorded voice intro into text for your profile bio, entirely on this device.”

Both are optional. If you decline either one, you can type your bio instead.

Live matchmaking

Live matchmaking uses Game Center to pair you with another person who is online right now. When you connect, the app sends that one person your display name and bio so they know who they are talking to. The icebreaker messages you exchange travel over Game Center's own connection between the two devices and are held in memory only — they are not saved to your device's database, are not written to CloudKit, and are discarded when the session ends. If you both then agree to turn it into a real match, the invite to the shared conversation zone described above is sent over that same direct connection.

Photos

Attaching a photo to a chat uses Apple's system photo picker. The app receives only the single image you pick; it has no access to the rest of your photo library, which is why it never asks for photo library permission.

What the app does not use at all

No camera, no location, no contacts, no address book, no health data, no calendar, no background tracking. The app requests none of those permissions because nothing in it needs them.

Purchases

In-app purchases are processed entirely by Apple. We are told that a purchase succeeded so the app can unlock; we never see your payment details. Purchase state is managed with RevenueCat, a third-party purchase infrastructure SDK. It is set up without an account identifier, so it works from an anonymous id it generates itself — the app does not pass it your name, your bio, your email address, your Game Center identity or anything you write in the app.

What we never collect

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no SDK that profiles you or builds an ad audience from you. We do not sell or share your data. This was verified against the source rather than assumed: the only third-party component in the app is the RevenueCat purchase SDK described above, and there is no analytics or advertising framework anywhere in it.

Children

This is a dating app intended for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their personal information.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes your profile, your matches and your messages from that device. Content already synced into a shared conversation zone stays in the participants' iCloud accounts until it is removed there, and your community-vetting records live in the public CloudKit database described above. Email us and we will delete your applicant, approval, flag and invite-code records.
